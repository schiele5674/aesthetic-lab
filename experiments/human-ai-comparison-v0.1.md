# Human-AI Comparison v0.1

## 1. Purpose

This analysis compares human aesthetic judgments with AI-generated goal alignment scores across four selected images and two design goals.

The purpose is to identify preliminary agreement and disagreement patterns between human and AI evaluations.

## 2. Comparison Data

AI scores are the average of GPT-6 and DeepSeek ratings for each image-goal pair.

*Human-AI Difference = Human Score − AI Average.*

## 3. Preliminary Observations

### 3.1 Overall Agreement

The human scores and AI average scores show relatively small numerical differences across the eight image-goal pairs.

The mean absolute difference is 0.4375 points on the 1–5 scale.

This indicates preliminary numerical proximity, but it does not establish that AI judgments are equivalent to human judgments.

### 3.2 Cases with Higher Human Ratings

For IMG07 × G01 and IMG08 × G01, the human evaluator assigned scores one point higher than the AI average.

These cases may be useful for further qualitative examination of differences in how human and AI evaluators interpret cultural expression, visual atmosphere, and goal alignment.

### 3.3 Cases with Similar Ratings

IMG02 × G02, IMG05 × G01, and IMG07 × G02 received identical human and AI average scores.

This agreement is descriptive and should not be interpreted as evidence of general model reliability.

## 4. Methodological Limitations

- Only one human evaluator participated.
- The human evaluation is exploratory rather than an expert consensus benchmark.
- The IMG02 human ratings were submitted after exposure to AI evaluations. They should not be treated as fully independent or blind judgments.
- AI average scores combine two models with potentially different scoring tendencies.
- The sample contains only four images and two design goals.

## 5. Preliminary Conclusion

The comparison reveals several points of numerical agreement as well as specific cases where human and AI judgments differ.

The differences are not necessarily model errors. They may reflect different interpretations of visual evidence, design goals, or scoring standards.

Further research should include more images, multiple independent human evaluators, and a more controlled blind evaluation procedure.

The current results should therefore be interpreted as preliminary observations rather than general conclusions about human or AI aesthetic judgment.
